## Reading skills for the 13 RC slots (arg-* on argument slots)

Write each slot's question as its variant (the form of the skill earlier mocks used least); a skill drawn twice gets two. Put a variant on a slot whose format fits it (an EXCEPT variant on a single-answer rc slot).

- **rc-select-verify** ×2 (review, wrong on a question testing it; wildcard, wrong on a question testing it): Each statement checked independently against the text (select-all or EXCEPT). Traps: a true-in-the-world but unsupported choice; one half-right choice. Variants: select-all inference about a claim the passage makes; select-all about a scholar or group the passage cites.
- **rc-function** (review, wrong on a question testing it): Why a sentence, example or paragraph is there (to concede, illustrate, qualify, contrast). Traps: an accurate paraphrase that misstates its role. Variant: what a stated fact is offered as evidence of.
- **rc-inference** ×2 (review, wrong on a question touching it; new, wrong on a question touching it): What must be true or is most strongly supported, including what a scholar or group the passage cites believes or would agree with. Traps: plausible but unsupported, true in the world but not in the passage, extreme wording, the author's view given for a cited party's. Variants: what a scholar or group the passage cites believes or would agree with; what the author would most likely agree with.
- **rc-vocab-context** (review, wrong on a question testing it): What a word or phrase means as used here. Traps: the word's common sense where the passage uses a secondary one. Variant: a figurative phrase as used in the passage.
- **rc-main-idea** (review, wrong on a question testing it): The passage's central claim, or why the author wrote it. Traps: too narrow (one paragraph's point), too broad, right topic with the wrong verb ("refute" vs "qualify"). Variant: the best summary of the passage as a whole.
- **rc-structure** (review, wrong on a question testing it): How the passage or a paragraph is built (claim then counterexample, two views then a synthesis). Traps: one move out of order or missing. Variant: the relationship between two ideas (spells out, applies, or contrasts with the other).
- **rc-application** (new, wrong on a question testing it): Which new situation parallels, would be explained by, or would be judged by the passage's idea. Variant: a sequence of events that would fit a theory the passage describes.
- **rc-attitude** (new, wrong on a question touching it): The author's stance and degree of certainty toward a view or person. Traps: tone too strong ("scathing") or the opposite polarity; a view the author only reports, or floats as speculation, read as one the author holds. Variant: a mixed or measured judgment of a work or person.
- **rc-detail** (new, right but slow): What the passage says explicitly. Traps: right words from the wrong part of the passage, distortions of degree or scope. Variant: a cause or sequence of events the passage states.
- **arg-complete** (new, never tested): Which statement most logically fills the blank that ends the passage, often after "since" or "because", so the key is the missing premise that makes the conclusion follow. Traps: a choice that restates the conclusion, or supports a neighbouring claim. Variant: the prediction that follows after therefore ___.
- **arg-role** (review, wrong on a question testing it): What part a highlighted statement plays: conclusion, premise, counter-premise, intermediate conclusion. Variant: two boldface portions (conclusion, evidence or counterclaim).
